Biblioteka Lounge — design brief

A demo slice of a website for a hookah lounge in Tbilisi that has none. Shown to the owner in person, on a phone, in a dim noisy lounge, Friday 2026-08-22. The owner gives it five seconds before deciding whether to keep listening. The pitch: a three-year Friday regular offers to finish the site for a symbolic price payable as a bar deposit. The demo is the argument — it must look finished even where it is shallow.

The venue, on record

  • Biblioteka Lounge — the name is written in Latin on all three language pages, never transliterated.
  • Hookah bar with a Belarusian kitchen — borscht, draniki, mangal meat — which no other lounge in Tbilisi claims. There is a fireplace. Books and library murals on the walls.
  • 4.8★ over 622 Google reviews; 30–80 GEL per person.
  • Aleksandre Abasheli St 1, Tbilisi. Mon–Thu & Sun 14:00–02:00, Fri–Sat 14:00–03:00. Phone +995 551 76 60 60.
  • Instagram @lounge.ge; booking via Telegram bot @Biblio_lounge_bot (deep link), phone as fallback.
  • Existing logo, seen on their own slide: a fire in a brick hearth arch used as the dot in BIBLI●TEKA LOUNGE. The owner of the pitch approved the hearth-as-O device outright.

One page, three languages

/ English (default), /ka/ Georgian, /ru/ Russian — full static copies of the same page, same sections, same order. Type must genuinely carry Georgian, Latin and Cyrillic; Georgian runs long and has no capitals, Russian runs wide. Sections in scroll order: hero (name, one line of identity, the street, the hours, booking button — the hours line may never say "tonight": the page is built once and read with scripts off, so a line naming a weekday state is wrong on every other day), atmosphere (3–5 photos, one concrete sentence each), menu-preview (signature items with GEL prices from a dated snapshot — hookah first, then the Belarusian kitchen, then the bar — one link to the live menu), visit (address, map link, hours, phone, Instagram, booking CTA repeated). No JavaScript required to read anything.

Hard floors — measured at review, not negotiable

  • Text contrast ≥ 4.5:1 (≥ 3:1 at 24px+/19px-bold+).
  • Every touch target ≥ 44px in both dimensions.
  • No horizontal scroll at 375px. Phone-first; desktop is a stretched reading of the same decisions.
  • First screen under 150 KB before photos — webfonts count, and are what broke this floor once: measure every woff2 subset the page actually requests, HTML included. Practical consequences: at most two weights per family, no italic axis unless italic is used, and a script subset is loaded only where the page's own copy needs it — a language label or a single foreign menu line falls back to the system stack instead of dragging a whole alphabet.
  • Photos lazy below the fold; the one image above it carries the page's weight budget, so it is the frame that must earn it.
  • No horizontal scroll and no orphaned separator down to 320px — 360px is the commonest Android width and 375 is not the floor.

The owner's vetoes — each one already cost an iteration

  • No amber/orange accent on near-black.
  • No text laid over a photo — the hero may not be caption-on-picture.
  • No hospitality clichés — every line concrete enough that only this venue could say it ("homemade borscht" was rejected with "whose home?").
  • Illustration over weak photography is welcome: schematic drawings carrying the venue's vibe instead of a mediocre photo.
  • The quality bar is explicitly above "low-mid okay": considered, confident, art-directed — not a template with the right colors.

Photos — borrowed from the venue's public Instagram

The owner of the pitch rates them "not their best", so the design must carry atmosphere even where a photo is weak. The strongest: the fireplace table (hero candidate), the two library murals, the borscht with the typewriter, the fortune-cookie slide carrying the venue's own logo. All 13 usable frames are in the repository under design/photos-inbox/, named by content. The layout may not depend on a crop a real photo cannot give. They are Instagram originals — one frame is 2.2 MB at 3000×3000 — so the frame chosen above the fold is a weight decision as much as a taste one.

Deliverables

  1. A design system: palette with hex, type pairing and ramp (all three scripts), spacing scale, and the core components — buttons, the hours row, a menu price line, a photo card — each drawn as a preview page.
  2. Mockups of the full page at 375px in all three languages, plus the edge cases the design must survive, assembled into a contact sheet.
